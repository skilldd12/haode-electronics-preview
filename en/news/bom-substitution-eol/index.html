When a BOM carries an end-of-life part: how to run the substitution check

Published 2026-10-14 Author HAODE 技术资料组

Confirm the status, not the rumour

Manufacturer lifecycle states run from active through not-recommended-for-new-designs, last-time-buy and obsolete. Each one calls for a different action. When a supplier says a part is gone, the first step is the manufacturer product page, not a substitute search.

Write down what must stay identical

Substitution is about constraints, not names. The items usually fixed are package and pinout, supply range, temperature grade, the critical timing or bandwidth figure, and qualification level for automotive or medical builds. Everything else is negotiable.

Compare against the datasheet, not the headline

Headline figures are usually measured under the most favourable conditions. For an op-amp, check the load behind the noise and settling numbers. For a MOSFET, check the VGS that the RDS(on) figure assumes. For memory, check the speed grade behind the timing table. Identical packages with different die areas are routine.

Validate in small volume before committing

A substitute has to run on a real board. Even a perfect parameter match leaves wetting, thermal cycling and long-term supply to be answered by hardware. Keep the first batch to prototype coverage.

Keep a substitution record

Which part, why it changed, what was verified, who signed off. When the next end-of-life notice lands, that record is the fastest starting point.
